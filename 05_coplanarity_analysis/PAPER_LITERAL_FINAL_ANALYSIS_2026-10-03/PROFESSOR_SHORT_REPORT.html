Short report · 3 October 2026

Reproducing Table I of Knapen, Lou, Papucci & Setford, “Tracking down Quirks at the LHC” (arXiv:1708.02243)

We used only settings written in the paper or in its detector reference [27] (ATLAS Inner Detector performance note). Nothing was tuned toward the paper's numbers. Each simulated sample is reported separately.

How to read the numbers. k/n = events passing / events tested. 95% CI = Wilson 95% interval. When no event passes, we give the one-sided 95% upper limit (UL; Clopper–Pearson).
The paper publishes no event counts and no uncertainties. The p-value is the exact binomial probability of our count if the paper's value were exactly true. p < 0.05 means the difference is statistically significant.
Signal region (paper Sec. IV): a reconstructed plane; 2 hits in 7 layers and at least 1 in the 8th; Δw < 1 cm; Δs < 0.01 cm.

Why the 4-of-8 rate is higher
  • The paper uses the full pile-up detector simulation of arXiv:1612.00850 (energy loss, bremsstrahlung, endcap material), which removes many low-momentum tracks. Ours propagates ideal helices through the 8 barrel layers only.
  • The paper drew from 125 000 minimum-bias events; our pool has 20 000.
  • The paper does not define whether "a plane" means a seed or a fitted candidate.
  • None of this affects the conclusion: no pile-up event reaches the signal region.

Notes and reasons
  • A 1% conversion probability is used, taken from the paper's own "~1 pb → ~10 fb". The PDG curves it cites need the beam-pipe material, which is not given.
  • Pile-up adds hits that fill the gaps, so 48 conversion + pile-up events pass the hit-count cut. All 48 then fail Δs; the closest has Δs = 121 µm against the 100 µm cut.
  • The cross-section difference cannot be traced: the paper does not give its generator settings. Yields are shown for both values; the paper's 1 pb is never forced.
  • Important: with the "no beam-axis smearing" detector that brings the signal close to Table I (see 3 and 4), 22/9139 = 0.24% [0.16, 0.36]% conversions pass the full signal region (≈ 1.5–7 events at 300 fb⁻¹). That detector choice is therefore not background-free.

Why reconstruction does not match (colored and EW)
  • The paper says SCT hits are smeared by "twice the intrinsic accuracy". Ref. [27] gives 17 µm across and 580 µm along the beam, so the literal smearing is 34 µm and 1.16 mm.
  • A 1.16 mm smear makes the fitted plane "thick": our signal Δs median is 110–160 µm, above the 100 µm cut, so most events fail.
  • The paper's own Fig. 5 shows a signal Δs median of only ~30 µm. That is only possible with ≲ 34 µm smearing, or none. The paper's text and its figures disagree, and it does not say what was actually simulated.
  • With no beam-axis smearing, the same events give 81–82% (EW) and 36% (COL2000), close to the paper. This value is not in the paper, so it is shown only as a diagnostic.
  • An independent sample with fresh seeds, set up before generation, confirms the literal result: EW 14/102 = 14%, colored 6/94 = 6%.
Why the EW trigger does not match
  • The same trigger definition (pT of the quirk pair > 200 GeV) reproduces the colored value (24%), so the definition itself is correct.
  • We tested 36 one-at-a-time generator changes (MadGraph 2.3.3 / Pythia 8.212 named in the paper source, matching scale, PDF, scales, tune, up to 2 jets, 14 TeV). The EW trigger stays at 13–19%; none gives 10%.
  • Our EW/colored ratio is 0.60–0.84; the paper's is 0.42. Some undocumented generator setting or extra requirement in the paper is the likely cause.
  • Fixing the EW model's Z coupling to vector-like (as the paper states) changes the cross-section by −24% but not the trigger (17.0%).

Conclusion. Following the paper literally, we reproduce the colored trigger efficiency, both fiducial efficiencies, and the paper's statement that no background survives the signal region. The EW trigger and both reconstruction efficiencies do not reproduce. A 100% reproduction of Table I is not possible from the published information. The detector resolution the paper actually used and its generator settings are not documented, and the paper gives no event counts or uncertainties. Matching the reconstruction efficiencies requires removing the SCT beam-axis smearing. That choice contradicts the paper's text and admits about 0.24% of photon conversions into the signal region, so we report it only as a systematic, not as the reproduction.
