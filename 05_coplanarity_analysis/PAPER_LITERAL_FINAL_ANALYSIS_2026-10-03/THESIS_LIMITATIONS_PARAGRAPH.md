The reproduction of Knapen et al. (arXiv:1708.02243) is limited first by information the paper does not provide, then by the size of our simulated samples, and finally by deliberate simplifications of the detector and background models.

**Undocumented information.** The paper publishes no raw event counts and no uncertainties for its Table I efficiencies, and it does not state its generator settings: matching scale, PDF set, renormalisation and factorisation scales, tune, or random seeds. Our efficiencies can therefore only be compared with the paper's central values, treated as exact. A sample-size-aware test relies on paper sample sizes inferred from the binning of its Fig. 5 histograms (about 1075 electroweak and 525 coloured reconstructed events), which are an inference rather than published numbers. More importantly, the paper's text and its own figures disagree about the strip-detector (SCT) resolution along the beam axis. Reference [27] quotes 580 µm along the beam, and applying the paper's "twice the intrinsic accuracy" to it gives 1.16 mm. The paper, however, never says which coordinate it doubled, and the distributions in its Fig. 5 are only consistent with a resolution of a few tens of µm or none at all. Because this single setting changes the electroweak reconstruction efficiency from about 0.15–0.31 (with 0.58–1.16 mm) to about 0.81–0.85 (without any smearing), a 100% reproduction of Table I is not possible from the published information.

**Unexplained differences.** The electroweak trigger efficiency (0.17–0.18 in our samples versus 0.10 in the paper) could not be reproduced by any of 36 documented one-at-a-time generator variations, including the generator versions named in the paper's source comments. The ratio of the electroweak to the coloured trigger efficiency remains 0.60–0.84, against 0.42 in the paper. Three other points are inconsistent or ambiguous within the paper itself:
- the stated "|η| ≲ 1.8" acceptance is not equivalent to the finite barrel lengths of its reference detector, and with the stated acceptance our electroweak fiducial efficiency is 0.45–0.51, against the paper's 0.28; only the reference lengths reproduce 0.28;
- the plane width plotted in its Fig. 4 is half of the Eq. 11 statistic;
- the 45 mm beam-spot smearing can be read as one common shift per event or as an independent shift of every hit, and only the first leaves any reconstructable plane.

The first and third change our Table I numbers and are shown as alternatives; the second changes no count.

**Statistical limitations.** After the trigger and fiducial requirements, only 6–62 signal events remain per sample. Reconstruction efficiencies therefore carry Wilson 95% intervals up to ±0.3, and the smallest coloured sample (6 events) cannot discriminate between hypotheses. The background samples (5000 pile-up events, 9139 photon conversions) can only bound the signal-region background at the level of 6×10⁻⁴ (pile-up) and 3.3×10⁻⁴ (conversions) per event at 95% confidence. This is far above the 6×10⁻⁵ level discussed in the paper.

**Modelling simplifications.** The pile-up detector simulation propagates exact helices through the eight barrel layers, without the energy loss, bremsstrahlung and endcap material of the simulation the paper cites. It draws its minimum-bias events from a pool of 20 000 events rather than 125 000. These differences probably explain why our rate of accidental four-layer planes is about six times the paper's 10⁻³. Our Z(νν)γ+jet cross-section (0.23 pb) is a factor of about four below the paper's "∼1 pb", for reasons that cannot be traced without the paper's generator settings. The conversion probability of 1% is derived from the paper's own numbers, because the beam-pipe material needed to read it from the cited PDG figures is not given. Finally, the electroweak signal model used SM-like chiral Z couplings instead of the vector-like couplings the paper specifies. A dedicated cross-check showed that this changes the cross-section by about 24% but leaves every Table I efficiency unchanged within statistical precision.
